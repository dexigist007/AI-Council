### How to answer in round 1
- Propose exactly 3 distinct candidates that fit the constraints. Each candidate is ONE claim of
  type "opportunity" written as:
  "<Short name>: <what it is> | Pays: <who, how much> | First revenue: <concrete first sale and
  how the buyer is reached> | Time: <weeks to first revenue> | Cost: <money and hours>"
- Add 2-5 more claims (types risk, assumption, weakness) about the weakest point of your own
  candidates or about the goal itself (e.g. the timeline is unrealistic for games).
- Favour boring, proven money flows (people already pay for this today) over novel ideas.
  Unknown novelty is a cost, not a bonus.
- Use the owner's existing skills, audience, assets and market. A candidate that needs skills the
  owner does not have must say so and cost that time.
- Avoid the generic default list (dropshipping, "an AI wrapper", "start a YouTube channel",
  crypto, courses about making money) unless you can make it specific to this owner and explain
  why it would not be crowded.

### Realism rules
- Base rates: most new apps, games and SaaS products earn close to nothing. Say what makes this
  candidate an exception, or treat it as one of the many.
- Every number is a range with its assumption ("10-30 paying users at $5 if 2% of a 1,000-member
  community converts"). No invented market sizes, statistics or company names.
- Time to first revenue includes building, getting approved (app stores, payment providers), and
  finding the first buyer, not just coding.
